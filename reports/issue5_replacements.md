## Methods replacement

For an offline demonstration, we applied the frozen box-plus-speed BiLSTM from the event-anchored controlled comparison to three short segments from two recorded PIE set03 test videos. The five best-validation-AUC checkpoints (seeds 42, 0, 1, 2, and 3) and their saved training-split normalization statistics were kept fixed. YOLO26m detected pedestrians and ByteTrack associated detections across frames. For each track, the most recent 16 contiguous detector boxes, expressed as original-frame coordinates (x1, y1, x2, y2), were paired with frame-aligned OBD ego speed in km/h. A track gap reset the input buffer. We averaged the five sigmoid probabilities and applied the controlled comparison's validation-selected threshold of 0.4579877. We verified the assembled ensemble on the recorded test windows before processing video. The three segments were selected from ground-truth pedestrian labels and OBD speed before inspecting predictions, to show a pre-onset crossing, a stationary non-crossing pedestrian, and a moving non-crossing pedestrian. Ground-truth annotations were used for selection and interpretation only. Faces were blurred in the exported figure. This demonstration was not used to select checkpoints or thresholds or to estimate test-set performance.

## Results replacement

The assembled ensemble exactly reproduced the controlled comparison's test results (2,094 event-anchored windows: F1 = 0.8313 at the frozen threshold, ROC AUC = 0.9320, PR AUC = 0.8768). Figure X shows newly generated detector-and-tracker outputs. In the stationary crossing example, the target received probability 0.9383 at video frame 5720, 46 frames before its first annotated crossing state at frame 5766. A stationary non-crossing target received 0.8430 and was falsely flagged. At 28.0 km/h, a non-crossing target received 0.0078 and was not flagged. At the displayed frames, 2/2, 2/2, and 0/7 tracked pedestrians were flagged in the respective scenes. Across the three selected 111-frame segments, 826 pedestrian-frame prediction records were produced from 23 segment-scoped tracker IDs. Probability and ego speed had a descriptive pedestrian-frame Pearson correlation of r = −0.970. At exactly 0 km/h, 300/300 records were flagged; above 20 km/h, 0/477 were flagged. These percentages have pedestrian-frame records as denominators and do not represent percentages of unique pedestrians or the complete PIE test-video collection.

## Discussion replacement

The offline scenes illustrate a strong dependence of this frozen predictor's output on ego speed and expose a stationary non-crossing false alert. Because the three segments were deliberately chosen by speed and annotation, and successive records from a tracked person are dependent, the pooled correlation and flagging percentages are descriptive of these processed segments only. They cannot establish speed causality, generalize to all PIE video, or replace the controlled test-set and ablation results. Detector-box and tracker errors introduce an additional shift from the annotated-box evaluation. The advance-prediction claim applies only to the highlighted crossing example, whose complete 16-frame observation window ends before the annotated onset.
